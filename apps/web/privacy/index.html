AnyGamepad

Privacy

Last changed on 14 October 2026.

The tester runs in your browser and sends nothing about you or your controllers anywhere. A device report leaves your device only when you share it, and sharing means posting it yourself as a public GitHub issue.

Who is responsible

anygamepad is an open-source project run by the maintainers of the anygamepad organization on GitHub. They decide what the project does with data: under the GDPR, they are its controller.

Questions and requests go to the project's issues. For anything private, open an issue that only asks to be contacted privately, without details, and a maintainer will answer with a private channel.

The tester in your browser

  • Your controllers. The page reads them through your browser's Gamepad API, which shows a gamepad only after you press one of its buttons, and through WebHID, only for a device you pick in the browser's chooser. What it reads stays in the page: it is shown, never sent. Your browser remembers the devices you allowed through WebHID; you can remove them in the site's settings.
  • Nothing from other sites. The page loads its files from anygamepad.github.io only: no analytics, no ads, no fonts or scripts from elsewhere, and no cookies.
  • Your theme. If you pick Light or Dark, this browser remembers it in its local storage (anygamepad.theme); Auto removes it.
  • Activity log and recordings. The activity log stays in the tab, and is gone when you close it unless you download it. "Record 2 seconds" saves a file on your device. The project receives neither unless you send it yourself.
  • Saved reports. When you save a report, this browser keeps it (in IndexedDB), with when you saved it and whether you copied it to share. You can look at, download and delete your reports at any time. Private windows drop them when they close, and Safari may delete them after seven days without a visit.

The desktop app

Once released, the desktop app works the same way: it saves reports as files in its data folder, only when you ask, and sends nothing by itself.

What a device report holds

A report describes a controller, not you:

  • how the controller identifies itself (vendor and product IDs, names, version) and how each browser or system API sees it;
  • samples of the data it sends and, through WebHID or the desktop app, the structure of that data;
  • the buttons you confirmed in the guided identification, and what you say about the product and its mode;
  • the app's version, your operating system (and its version, from the desktop app), your browser's engine and major version, and the day (UTC) the report was made.

It never holds serial numbers, Bluetooth addresses, device paths, your name or account, your IP address or the time of day. Texts that look like identifiers are replaced with [redacted], and the apps show you the whole report before you share it. The format is documented in the repository.

Sharing a report

"Share" copies a report and opens a GitHub issue form; nothing is shared until you submit the form on GitHub. Then:

  • The issue is public: anyone can read the report and see the GitHub account that posted it. GitHub handles your account and the issue under its Terms of Service and Privacy Statement.
  • You dedicate the report to the public domain under CC0 1.0, so that anyone can use it, and the anygamepad library can build its controller profiles from it. The form asks you to confirm this, and that you reviewed the report.
  • The maintainers read shared reports to improve how controllers are detected. Changes made from a report refer to its issue by number, not to your account.
  • Without a GitHub account, you can download a report instead, and keep it.

How long data is kept

  • In your browser or on your computer: until you delete it. The project can neither see nor delete it.
  • Shared reports: the issue stays open until a maintainer has used the report, then it is closed and kept, as the public source of the data. It is deleted when you ask, within a month, or as soon as a maintainer sees personal data in it.
  • Controller profiles and test data made from reports stay in the repository. They hold no personal data.
  • Copies outside the project, such as GitHub's backups and notification emails, or copies other people made of a public report, are out of its reach.

The website's host

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security purposes, whether or not they are signed in to GitHub. GitHub is responsible for those logs; the project has no access to them.

No server yet

There is no anygamepad server: nothing is uploaded anywhere. A server to synchronize reports may come later. Before it does, this page will say what it stores, and the apps will ask you before uploading any report. It will not store IP addresses.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to the data the project holds about you, for its correction or deletion, or to restrict its use; you can object to that use, and take the data with you. The only personal data the project handles is the GitHub account on the issues you posted, so the usual request is to delete such an issue: ask on the issue itself, or in a new issue that links it, from the same account. This also withdraws your consent, which does not affect what was done before. Requests are answered within a month. You can also complain to the data protection authority of your country.

Changes

The history of this page is in the repository. If a change makes the project collect or share more, the tester will say so before it applies.
