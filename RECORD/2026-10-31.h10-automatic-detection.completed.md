# H10: Automatic detection

- Status: completed
- Roadmap: ROADMAP/2026-10-31/ (task ID: H10)

## Goal

What the test modes tell by themselves (owner, 2026-10-31): which of the
connected pads the person means, which catalog model, mode and transport
it is, which unit of the lab, and whether a pad picked from the list is
the one that connected.

## Done

- `anygamepad_db::detect`:
  - `Catalog::identify(identity, inventory)`: `Candidate`s (model, mode,
    transport or `None` to ask, specificity, `in_inventory`), most
    specific first, then models the lab has units of, then catalog order;
    the generic model of the device's family when nothing matches
    (`generic_model_id`).
  - `Catalog::check_choice(identity, model, mode, transport, inventory)`:
    `Matches`, `NotKnown` (no identity in the catalog yet, or a generic
    model), `Differs` (with what the device looks like instead) or
    `NoSuchChoice`.
  - `Inventory::proposed_units(model, last_picked)`: the units of a model,
    the one last picked on this machine first.
  - `transports_fit`: same transport, or Bluetooth and Bluetooth LE
    (most providers cannot tell them apart); an unknown transport fits
    any.
- `anygamepad::detect::PressedPad`: among several pads, the one pressed
  (a button past half its travel, a hat off centre, an axis half its range
  from rest, with the wizard's thresholds), only once the pad was seen at
  rest; follows slow drift; `forget` on disconnect.
- Tests: every catalog identity identifies its model, mode and transport;
  a DualSense, a Switch Pro Controller and an Xbox Series pad over
  Bluetooth (seen as Bluetooth, catalogued as LE); the Gamepad API
  without transport (asked for the DualSense, known for the wired Xbox
  360); generic fallbacks (an unknown Microsoft pad, an unknown vendor,
  a browser XInput pad); a multi-mode test pad sharing `045e:028e` and
  `057e:2009` with the catalog, inventory first; a picked mode against a
  pad still in another mode; units last picked first; the pressed pad
  among two, a pad held since it connected, misfit states and NaN.
  Checked: fmt, clippy (db with all features, anygamepad with and
  without default features, wasm32), rustdoc, tests, Rust 1.85.

## Decisions

- Candidates are per model and mode, not per connection; their transport
  is the device's when it tells, the mode's only matching transport
  otherwise, or `None` for the person to say. Reason: the Gamepad API
  hides the transport, and a DualSense shows the same IDs over USB and
  Bluetooth.
- Specificity first, then inventory, then catalog order. Reason: a more
  specific identity (a name, a descriptor hash) beats a shared VID/PID;
  among pads that share one (`045e:028e`), the lab's own come first.
- A device whose transport the catalog connection does not offer is not
  that connection. Reason: an Xbox 360 wired controller over Bluetooth is
  something else.
- The last unit picked per model is the app's to keep, not a report's.
  Reason: it is local convenience, and reports stay anonymous.
- `PressedPad` does not wait for the pad to be still before learning its
  rest (the wizard waits 300 ms). Reason: it only needs released buttons
  and centred hats to tell a press, and the person is asked to press
  right away; drift is followed while nothing is used.

## Open questions

- Product names and descriptor hashes are not in the catalog yet, so
  shared VID/PIDs stay ambiguous until test reports add them (H9) and C8
  refines the matching.
